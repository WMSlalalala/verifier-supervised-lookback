\section{Conclusion}
The verifier that defines a delayed information need also labels which earlier screens answer it, so look-back trains on any corpus of agent trajectories without a person labeling anything. That supervision is the lever: it lifts a one-call full-history reader by 10.6 points and a per-screen scorer by 22.3, and it reverses which of the two architectures is better. The decomposition buys a further 6.9 points of localization and 2.1 end-to-end, neither of which a paired test separates, and 21.8 points at gaps of 8--12 screens, where one call must weigh many undifferentiated candidates. On a fourth sample both trained variants keep a 17--20 point localization margin over prompting (79.6\% and 76.3\%) and reach 49.5\% and 51.6\% end-to-end against 23.7\%, remaining indistinguishable from each other. Gaps of 13 or more stay open: both trained variants fall below prompted full history there.
